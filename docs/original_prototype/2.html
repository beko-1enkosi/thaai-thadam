Executive Summary for Judges

Thaai Thadam: Trichy's Safe Mobility Ecosystem

Problem: 79% of harassment against women in India occurs at first-and-last-mile transition points between transport modes. In Trichy — a temple town, university city, and industrial hub — women face unsafe walking routes, unlit bus stops, unverified auto drivers, and no emergency support.

Solution: Four integrated pillars — Safe Route Guidance, Smart Mobility Hubs, Verified Last-Mile Transport, and Emergency Assistance — delivered via a mobile app and low-cost physical upgrades at 15 pilot bus stops.

Implementation: 90-day pilot in Ward 12 (Chathiram Bus Stand corridor) with TNSTC Trichy partnership, 50 verified drivers, and Bharathidasan University as anchor institution.

Impact: Targets 5,000+ women, 40% reduction in last-mile costs, 60% improvement in perceived safety (precedent: UP My Safe Bus pilot), direct alignment with SDG 5, 11, and 16.

Local Context

Why Trichy? Why Now?

Trichy is not just any Tier-2 city. Its unique identity as a temple town, university hub, and industrial center creates distinct mobility challenges that generic solutions cannot address.

Trichy at a Glance

Trichy (Tiruchirappalli) is a Tier-2 city with ~1 million population, serving as the administrative headquarters of Trichy district. It hosts:

  • Bharathidasan University — 15,000+ students, majority women
  • KAP Viswanatham Government Medical College — Major healthcare employer
  • Golden Rock Railway Workshop — Large industrial workforce
  • BHEL Trichy — PSU with significant female employees
  • Srirangam Temple Corridor — High pedestrian density, narrow streets
  • Cantonment Market & Chathiram Bus Stand — Major commercial nodes

Trichy-Specific Pain Points

Unlike metro cities, Trichy's mobility challenges are shaped by its geography and land use:

  • Rockfort Area: Steep pedestrian congestion, narrow lanes, no stroller access
  • Srirangam Corridor: Temple-town chaos, irregular bus frequency, unsafe waiting areas
  • Cantonment Bazaar: Market crowding, no designated pedestrian zones, harassment hotspots
  • Last-Mile Gaps: Central Bus Stand to Thillai Nagar, Palkalaiperur to main campus
  • Night Safety: Poor lighting on Palkalaiperur Road, Kattur Road, and Srirangam approach
  • TNSTC Bus Stops: No real-time info, no seating, no shade, no emergency support

Local Stakeholder Ecosystem

Thaai Thadam is designed to integrate with Trichy's existing institutional framework:

  • TCMC (Tiruchirappalli City Municipal Corporation): Smart City funds, ward-level implementation
  • TNSTC Trichy: Bus fleet operator, route data, driver network
  • Auto-Rickshaw Unions: 15,000+ autos, partnership for verified driver pool
  • Bharathidasan University: Anchor institution for student ridership
  • Govt Hospitals & BHEL: B2B institutional partnerships for guaranteed ridership
  • Local NGOs & SHGs: Community ambassadors, awareness campaigns

The Challenge

Three Critical Barriers in Trichy

Women in Trichy face a "pink tax" on mobility — paying more in time, money, and safety for the same journeys. We identified three interconnected barriers directly mapped to the hackathon tracks.

Accessibility Gap

48%
of travel cost on 18% of distance

First-and-last-mile connectivity is the biggest cost and time burden. Women spend nearly half their travel budget on short auto rides from bus stops. No safe walking routes, no verified shared rides, and no real-time bus information.

Sensitivity Blindspot

84%
of women's work trips use public transport

Bus stops lack seating, shade, and stroller access. Infrastructure is designed for male commuting patterns, ignoring trip-chaining (work + childcare + errands). Caregivers with strollers and groceries face physical barriers at every stop.

Safety Crisis

95%
feel unsafe on public transport after dark

Unlit streets, isolated bus stops, unverified auto drivers, and no emergency support. Harassment is normalized — women don't report due to fear of shame and zero trust in police response. Safety infrastructure stops at station boundaries.

The Pink Tax on Mobility

Women in Trichy pay more, wait longer, and travel less safely than men for the same destinations. Lower-income women are most affected — forced to choose between safety and affordability, between employment and caregiving.

Our Solution

Four Integrated Pillars

Thaai Thadam integrates digital tools, physical infrastructure, community participation, and policy alignment into one coordinated ecosystem. Each pillar maps directly to a hackathon theme and UN SDG target.

01

Safe Route Guidance

AI-powered routing prioritizing well-lit streets, avoiding isolated areas, with real-time safety scores. Women vote on priority routes — crowdsourced demand drives where smart hubs and verified drivers deploy next.

Well-lit Paths Safety Scoring Community Reports Crowdsourced Routes
SDG 11.2 — Safe transport for all
02

Smart Mobility Hubs

Low-cost physical upgrades at bus stops: solar lighting (30-40 lux), seating, shade, QR codes for real-time info, clear sightlines, and public toilets. More than a bus stop — a shared community space with "eyes on the street."

Solar Lighting QR Codes Clear Sightlines Community Space
SDG 5.4 — Recognize unpaid care work
03

Verified Last-Mile Transport

Partnership with auto unions and women driver initiatives (Rapido Pink, MOWO model). GPS-registered drivers, displayed ratings, safe shared rides. B2B partnerships with colleges and hospitals guarantee ridership.

Driver Verification Women Drivers Shared Rides B2B Partnerships
SDG 16.1 — Reduce violence everywhere
04

Emergency Assistance & Data

One-tap SOS with live location sharing, quick dial to police/ambulance/women's helpline. Gender-tagged electronic ticketing (SADDD data) captures boarding patterns to inform service planning and policy.

One-Tap SOS Live Location SADDD Data Gender Tagging
SDG 5.2 — Eliminate violence against women

Implementation

90-Day Sprint to Launch

Implementation-ready from day one. Our phased approach minimizes risk while maximizing learning — designed for real-world execution in Trichy's Ward 12 corridor.

Partnerships & MVP

Secure MOUs with TNSTC Trichy, auto unions, and Bharathidasan University. Develop app MVP. Select 15 pilot bus stops in Ward 12 (Chathiram Bus Stand to Thillai Nagar corridor).

Pilot Deployment

Install QR codes, solar lighting, and seating at pilot stops. Launch verified driver network. Begin SADDD data collection. Conduct first quarterly gender audit with community participation.

Data & Refinement

Evidence Base

Research That Shapes Our Solution

Thaai Thadam is grounded in rigorous field research and proven interventions from across Indian cities and global best practices.

The "Pink Tax" on Mobility

Women spend 48% of travel costs on just 18% of distance — the first and last mile segments. Despite free bus schemes, women continue paying premium prices for perceived safer options.

— Beyond Free Rides: Multi-State Assessment, Nikore Associates & Sustainable Mobility Network, 2025

Safety at Transition Points

79% of women report harassment during transition points between transport modes. Safety infrastructure inside stations does not extend to surrounding areas where passengers wait for autos or walk to destinations.

— World Bank Toolkit for Gender Responsive Urban Mobility, 2022

Women Drivers = Safer Rides

Initiatives like Rapido Pink (2 lakh women captains) and MOWO (2,500+ trained women drivers) demonstrate that women-driven services create both employment and passenger safety. 80% of women report feeling safer with women drivers.

— Azad Foundation Annual Report 2024-25; MOWO Impact Data

Infrastructure Precedents

Chennai's NMT Policy (2014) dedicated 60% of transport budget to non-motorized infrastructure, resulting in 80% of women rating the city as safe. Bhubaneswar's Janpath Street redesign created safer walking environments.

— Corporation of Chennai; Bhubaneswar Development Authority

My Safe Bus Pilot

Uttar Pradesh's Nirbhaya-funded pilot deployed 50 pink buses with GPS, CCTV, and panic buttons. Result: 1.5 lakh women benefited, 60% rise in perceived safety, 40% increase in timely harassment reporting.

— UPSRTC Nirbhaya Fund Implementation, 2017

Trip Chaining Reality

Women's travel combines work, childcare, and household errands — requiring flexible schedules and frequent stoppages. 84% of women's work trips depend on public transport, yet systems are designed for male peak-hour commuting.

— World Bank; SPRF Women's Mobility Study, 2024
