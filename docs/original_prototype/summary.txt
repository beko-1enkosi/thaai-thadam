 THAAI THADAM — Safe Mobility Ecosystem for Women in Trichy

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Indian city infrastructure is designed for a "neutral male user," creating a hidden "pink tax" on women's mobility. Field research from Bhopal, Gwalior, and Jodhpur — tier-2 cities comparable to Trichy — confirms that 95% of women identify public transport as unsafe, the highest across all public spaces. Critically, 79% of harassment occurs during transition points between transport modes — the first and last mile gaps where women spend 48% of travel costs on just 18% of distance. Women make shorter, more frequent trips combining work, childcare, and errands (trip-chaining), yet systems are planned for male peak-hour commuting. 87% of low-income women walk as their only viable transport mode, but unsafe walking infrastructure forces them into transport poverty. Bus stops lack seating, shade, lighting, and real-time information. Auto drivers refuse meter fares. When harassment occurs, 100% of women in tier-2 cities do not report it to police due to normalization, shame, and zero trust. The result: severe time poverty, restricted economic participation, and daily fear.

PROPOSED SOLUTION

Thaai Thadam ("Mother's Footprint") is an eight-pillar safe mobility ecosystem integrating digital tools, pilot projects, awareness campaigns, and policy change:

1. Safe Route Guidance — AI-powered routing prioritizing well-lit streets with real-time safety scores and crowdsourced route voting
2. Smart Mobility Hubs — Solar-lit bus stops (30-40 lux per IRC 103:2012) with seating, shade, QR codes, public toilets with female attendants, and transparent panels for clear sightlines. Located near active commercial frontages per Jane Jacobs' "eyes on the street" principle
3. Verified Last-Mile Transport — GPS-registered auto partnerships including women drivers (Rapido Pink/MOWO model), with B2B institutional partnerships guaranteeing ridership
4. Emergency Assistance — One-tap SOS with live location sharing and quick-dial to police/ambulance/women's helpline
5. Fare Integration & SADDD Data — Sex-, Age-, Disability-Disaggregated Data through gender-tagged electronic ticketing, following CRUT Odisha's precedent
6. Request-a-Stop — Women can request drop-offs between designated stops after dark, proven across 5,000+ buses in Istanbul
7. Gender-Responsive Maintenance — Walkways, bike lanes, and transit routes prioritized before car roads (Karlskoga, Sweden precedent — traffic injuries halved)
8. Intersectional Approach — Gender Advisory Committee with 50%+ women including women with disabilities, trans* persons, and marginalized caste representatives

FEASIBILITY

The pilot budget is Rs. 3.55 lakhs for Months 1-3: QR codes and signage (Rs. 20,000), bus-stop lighting upgrades (Rs. 1,50,000), app prototype (Rs. 50,000), awareness campaign (Rs. 30,000), women driver onboarding (Rs. 25,000), SADDD data system (Rs. 15,000), request-a-stop training (Rs. 10,000), intersectional data collection (Rs. 12,000), active mobility infrastructure (Rs. 18,000), and contingency (Rs. 25,000). The phased rollout begins with 10-15 bus stops in one ward, expands to 50-100 stops by Month 12, and achieves city-wide adoption by Year 2. Stakeholders include TCMC (implementation), UNDP (SDG frameworks), ICLEI South Asia (climate alignment), Yi/CII (CSR funding and B2B partnerships), auto unions, and women commuters as co-design partners.

IMPACT

Targets: 5,000+ women and caregivers reached, 15 mobility hubs with community spaces, 100+ verified transport partners, and measurable improvements across gender, social, and environmental metrics. Gender metrics include felt-safety scores pre/post, SADDD ridership share, and harassment reports resolved within 24 hours. Social metrics include jobs for women drivers/attendants, trip-chaining time saved, and caregiver participation in planning. Environmental metrics track modal shift from private auto to bus/walk/cycle per the UNFCCC "Avoid-Shift-Improve" framework. The UP "My Safe Bus" precedent (Nirbhaya Fund, Rs. 83 crore) achieved a 60% rise in perceived safety — Thaai Thadam achieves similar outcomes at 1/234th the cost through QR codes, community verification, and gender-responsive maintenance. Aligned with SDG 5 (Gender Equality), SDG 11.2 (safe transport for all), and SDG 16 (peace and justice), Thaai Thadam moves beyond gender-sensitive to gender-transformative mobility — because every woman deserves to travel safely from the first step to the last.
